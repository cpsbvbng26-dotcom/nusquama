The King's errata

This is a work of fiction, written for fun. In these errata the King within the fiction records the King's own mistakes.

This is an English translation. The Japanese version governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies. The quotations below are translated from the Japanese.

As a frozen text it is not rewritten; its mistakes are recorded here (Precept 2). The text of the rules may be changed. When text is replaced or removed, the text before the change is kept here. How entries are recorded

Each entry gives the number, the date recorded, where, the wrong text, the right text, what it was checked against, the category and the place name.

  • The numbers run from 1 without gaps.
  • The date is the day the King admitted the mistake and recorded it.
  • "Where" is the page and section of the text that was wrong.
  • "Wrong" copies the wrong text as it was.
  • "Right" says what is right and how the text was changed.
  • "Checked against" names the source used to confirm the right text. When nothing was checked, it says so (Precept 1).
  • The category and the place name follow the rule under "Categories and place names" below.

What is recorded

The King's errata record only mistakes in the King's own texts. Mistakes in other people's texts are not recorded. Making someone else's mistake into territory would be judging that person. If an entry itself turns out to be wrong, the earlier row is not erased. A new row records that the earlier row was wrong (Institutions, "Pardon").

Reporting a mistake

Anyone may report a mistake in the King's texts through the form 誤りの知らせ (Report a mistake). There is no fee. A mistake the King confirms is recorded here as the King's own mistake. A report that cannot be confirmed is closed with a note saying so in the issue. These errata do not name the person who reported the mistake. Reporting a mistake of the King's does not meet the condition for knighthood (having publicly corrected a mistake of one's own).

A place name is the name of the isle followed by a Roman numeral for its order within the category (for example, Insula Facti I). The King does not name places by hand. Once the category is fixed, so is the name. The name of each isle is the Latin insula ("isle") followed by the genitive of a noun for the category. Their forms come from general knowledge and have not been checked in a dictionary.

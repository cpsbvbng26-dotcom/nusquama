The King's errata

This is a work of fiction, written for fun. In these errata the King within the fiction records his own mistakes.

This is an English translation. The Japanese version governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies. The quotations below are translated from the Japanese.

Under Article 5 of the Basic Law, the King does not erase his mistakes, and records them with a date. The Founding Declaration is a text issued with a date. As a frozen text it is not rewritten; its mistakes are recorded here (Precept 2). The text of the rules may be changed. When it is, the text before the change is kept here. These errata are the domain under Article 3 of the Basic Law.

How entries are recorded

Each entry gives the number, the date recorded, where, the wrong text, the right text, what it was checked against, the category and the place name.

  • The numbers run from 1 without gaps.
  • The date is the day the King admitted the mistake and recorded it.
  • "Where" is the page and section of the text that was wrong.
  • "Wrong" copies the wrong text as it was.
  • "Right" says what is right and how the text was changed.
  • "Checked against" names the source used to confirm the right text. When nothing was checked, it says so (Precept 1).
  • The category and the place name follow the rule under "Categories and place names" below.

What is recorded

The King's errata record only mistakes in texts the King wrote himself. Mistakes in other people's texts are not recorded. Making someone else's mistake into territory would be judging that person. If an entry itself turns out to be wrong, the earlier row is not erased. A new row records that the earlier row was wrong (Institutions, "Pardon").

Categories and place names

Mistakes are sorted into six categories. The map uses only the three colours of the emblem, so the categories are told apart by the shape of the parcel.

A place name is the name of the isle followed by a Roman numeral for its order within the category (for example, Insula Facti I). The King does not name places by hand. Once the category is fixed, so is the name. The names of the isles are Latin nouns in the genitive. Their forms come from general knowledge and have not been checked in a dictionary.

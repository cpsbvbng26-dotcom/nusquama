Institutions

This is a work of fiction, written for fun. The institutions here are rules of the kingdom within the fiction. They are not the bodies or procedures of any real state, and they have no legal effect on anyone.

This is an English translation. The Japanese version governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

This page arranges the Basic Law and the rules by how the kingdom works. Where they differ, the Basic Law and the rules apply.

All the bodies are held by the King alone. Besides the King there are no officials, no assembly and no court. The part of the King is played by the operator.

Proclamations and their numbers

Events of the kingdom appear in the Gazette with an issue number. Issues are numbered from 1, oldest first. The number of a proclamation is the number of the gazette issue that carries it. For example, the proclamation that laid down the Basic Law and the Ten Precepts is gazette No. 1.

A proclamation is written as one row of the timeline on the top page, in this form:

  • Date: the date in Japan time.
  • Event: one sentence on what was laid down or done.
  • English: the English translation of the event.

Errata and knightings reach the gazette from the rows of the errata and the roll. They are not written in the timeline.

Population and how people are called

The population is the number of knights of the Ordo Erratorum. The current population and the numbers by year are in the tables of For anyone introducing Nusquama. The tables are counted from the roll, not written by hand. Knights are not citizens. Nusquama gives no nationality (Rules on Knighting). Anyone who is not a knight is called a visitor. Visitors have nothing to register or report. The King is not counted in the population, because the King is not on the roll.

How decisions are made

The King decides. There are no votes and no elections. Knights have neither the duty nor the right to take part in decisions. A knight may leave at any time. The King's decisions are announced in the gazette or by the date of last update of a rule.

Pardon

Nusquama has no pardon that erases a mistake (Article 5 of the Basic Law). If a recorded entry itself turns out to be wrong, the earlier row is not erased. A new row records that the earlier row was wrong. So asking for a pardon makes the domain one parcel larger. Removing a knight's name from the roll is not a pardon. Leaving and revocation follow the Rules on Knighting.

Changing the rules

The Basic Law and the rules are changed by the King, in this order:

  1. Change the Japanese version, and on the same day the English translation.
  2. Change the date of last update of the rule.
  3. Where text is replaced or removed, keep the text before the change in the King's errata (as the opening of the errata says).
  4. Add a row to the timeline, so that it appears in the gazette.
  5. Publish only after the checks pass.

Articles 1, 6 and 8 of the Basic Law are not removed (Article 10). When a change takes effect and how it is announced follow the Terms of Use, "Changes and ending".

Versions

Nusquama does not number the versions of its rules. A rule is referred to by its date. Earlier rules can be read in the history of the public repository.

The yearly review

Every year on 7 November (Founding Day), a review issue opens automatically. The King follows it and checks that the rules and practice still agree.

  • Does every page say that this is fiction?
  • Is there no way to buy a title or to give a donation?
  • Has no knight's request to leave been left unanswered?
  • Is the watch on the laws still running?
  • Is the English translation up to date with the Japanese version?
  • Is the kingdom still unconnected with the operator's other activities?

Freezing

If the King can no longer keep the kingdom going, the kingdom is frozen. When it is frozen:

  • No requests for knighthood are accepted.
  • The roll, the errata and the rules stay public as they are.
  • Requests for deletion, correction or leaving are answered as far as possible.

The freezing is announced by putting the "Closing words" below on the top page.

Dissolution

To close the kingdom, the King follows this order:

  1. Close the form for requesting knighthood.
  2. Put up the closing words and announce them in the gazette.
  3. Tell the knights that they may ask to leave the roll, and do so for any who ask.
  4. Allow at least 30 days for such requests before stopping the site.
  5. Keep the repository, in a read-only state.

Personal data is handled as the Privacy page says.

Closing words

This is the draft of the text put up on freezing or dissolution. It is issued with the date of that day.

The kingdom that is nowhere returns to nowhere. The King's errata stay here. The mistakes are left behind, not erased. Knights, thank you for correcting mistakes with us.

Ten years ahead

Some things will not grow, even in ten years.

  • No land, currency, passports, certificates or objects will be made.
  • No ranks will be added. There is only one: knight.
  • Requests from people who live outside Japan, or who are outside Japan when applying, will not be accepted until the laws of that country have been checked.

Some things will go on.

  • Recording mistakes and widening the domain.
  • Keeping the rules in step with changes in the law.
  • Reviewing once a year.

On 7 November 2036, this outlook will be compared with what happened, and the result announced in the gazette.

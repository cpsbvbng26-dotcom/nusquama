The founding declaration

The declaration is a dated text, and it is not rewritten (Ten Precepts, II). The King's errata correct two of its statements, in entries 1 and 3 of the King's errata.

There is a country that is nowhere. Its name is Nusquama: in Latin, "a place that is nowhere". It is the name that Thomas More first nearly gave to the island that later became Utopia.

The country that is nowhere has a king who is no one. The King's name is Nemo: in Latin, "no one". The Knight-King Nemo I has no subjects. The one person the King rules is the King alone.

This King does not wield a sword. The King's weapon is the check, and the King's shield is the errata. The King's greatness is not measured by how few errors there are. It is measured by the honesty of a record that keeps its errors instead of erasing them.

The crown lasts one reign, and has no heir. For only those who stand alone can correct themselves alone.

Here the founding of the Knightly Kingdom of Nusquama (Regnum Nusquamae) is declared.

Nemo I, the Knight-King, 7 November 2026

The Basic Law of the Kingdom

Article 1 (Fiction) This country is a work of fiction. It is not related to any real state, royal house, order of knighthood, honour or title, and it has no territory, people, currency or passports.

Article 2 (The King) The King is one person only, Nemo I, the Knight-King (Nemo I, Rex Eques Nusquamae). The reign lasts one generation and is not inherited.

Article 3 (The domain) The domain of the Kingdom is the King's bookshelf and the errata that the King has made public. The domain widens each time the King admits an error.

Article 4 (The King's vows) The King does not silently withdraw a vow once made. When the King amends a vow, the King leaves a record of what was replaced by what.

Article 5 (The King's errors) The King does not erase the King's own errors, and records them with a date. An erased error counts as a second error.

Article 6 (Knighting) Knighting is free of charge. It is not given in exchange for money, goods or services. It has one condition: to have publicly corrected one of one's own errors.

Article 7 (The Order) Knights belong to the Order of Errata (Ordo Erratorum). There is one rank only, "knight", with none higher or lower.

Article 8 (Where the titles are used) The titles of this country are used only within this country. They are not written in a CV, a paper, on a business card, in an official document or in any academic setting.

Article 9 (Arms) The Kingdom does not draw its sword. Disputes are settled by words and evidence.

Article 10 (Amendment) The King amends this Basic Law. Articles 1, 6 and 8, however, are never removed.

The Ten Precepts of the Knight-King

I. Do not fill a gap with a guess. What you do not know, write that you do not know.

II. Do not rewrite frozen words. To correct them, write on a separate sheet.

III. If you speak of numbers, speak in a form that can be counted again.

IV. Do not adorn yourself with titles. The name of the Knight-King is allowed only within this country.

V. Take no money. A rank bought is no rank.

VI. Do not erase errors. Errors are the King's domain.

VII. One thing to a sentence. A sword, too, makes one cut to a stroke.

VIII. Do not raise your voice. Show severity by how much you cut away.

IX. Do not be ashamed of being alone. Only those who stand alone can correct themselves alone.

X. Put the King, too, to the check.

This is a work of fiction, written for fun. Nusquama is not a state and is not related to any real state, royal house, order of knighthood, honour or title. The knightly names it gives are not decorations, honours or ranks of knighthood, and have no legal effect.

Rules on Knighting

Last updated: 2026-11-12.

This is an English translation. The Japanese version governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

What knighting is

Knighting is giving the name of knight, within a work of fiction. It brings no legal effect, status, privilege or honour. Knighting does not confer a decoration, honour or rank of knighthood of any country. Knighting is free. It is never given in exchange for money, goods or services (Basic Law, Article 6). Knighting issues no document or object, such as a letter of appointment, a certificate or an insignia. It only puts an account name on the roll.

Who may apply

A person who has publicly corrected one of their own mistakes. The correction must be public and checkable by anyone, such as an erratum to a paper, a note added to a blog post or article, a fix to code, or a statement withdrawn. A person under 16 may not apply. A person whom the law of the country where they live forbids to receive or use a title of knighthood given by a private person may not apply either. For example, Article 8 of Italy's Law No. 178 of 1951 forbids private persons and bodies to confer titles of knighthood under any name. From 2026-11-22, only people who live in Japan and are in Japan when they apply may apply. Nationality does not matter, except that people who hold Italian nationality are excluded. From 2026-11-22, people who live outside Japan may not apply, whatever their nationality, because the laws outside Japan cannot all be checked country by country. From 2026-11-22, a person who lives in Japan may not apply while outside Japan, for travel, business or study, because some foreign laws reach not only those who live there but those who are there. From 2026-11-22, a person who holds Italian nationality may not apply, even if they live in Japan, and even if they also hold another nationality. There are two reasons. First, by plain common sense, an Italian national becoming a knight of a Japanese micronation is out of place to begin with. Second, Article 8 of Italy's Law No. 178 of 1951 also punishes anyone who uses such a title, and applies even when it was conferred abroad. An Italian national who received and used the title is the most realistic path by which that article could reach Nusquama. This limit reduces the possibility that the article applies; it does not remove it (Legality Statement, "Laws outside Japan"). A person who only stays in Japan briefly, such as a traveller, does not count as living in Japan.

The premise of good sense

From 2026-11-22, knighting presumes that the applicant is a person of good sense who treats the title as play. This premise means using the title only within the fiction, and keeping the following rules.

  • Use the title only within the kingdom (Terms of Use, "Titles"; Basic Law, Article 8).
  • Do not present the title as if it were a real title, decoration or insignia of an organisation.
  • Do not put the title on a CV, a paper, a business card, an official document, a business transaction or an advertisement.
  • Do not sell the title, and do not use it to collect money or goods.
  • Do not use the title to deceive anyone.

These set out, as ways of using the title, the rules in the Terms of Use ("Titles", "What is not allowed", "No ill intent, and obeying the law") and Article 8 of the Basic Law. From 2026-11-22, the applicant ticks a box in the form for this premise. If what the applicant wrote, or the linked correction, shows clearly that this premise does not hold, the applicant is not knighted ("What is declined"). If it becomes clear after knighting, the knighting is revoked under "Withdrawal and revocation". When revoking, the operator writes in the application issue only which rule was not kept, briefly, and comments on no one. The issue is deleted if the person asks.

How to apply

Vercel, which serves the site, and GitHub, which keeps the applications, may use what is placed on them to train AI models. If you are worried about your personal data, do not apply. These uses are described in Privacy.

  1. Open an issue with the form Application for knighthood.
  2. Give a public link to the correction, and say what was wrong and how you corrected it.
  3. Tick every one of the pledges.

What an applicant agrees to is the version of the rules on the day of the application. That version can be checked in the history of the public repository.

Do not write your real name, address, affiliation or contact details.

What the operator checks

  • That the link is public and reads as a correction.
  • That it reads as a correction of the applicant's own mistake.
  • That it contains no personal data of others and nothing that hurts others.
  • That every pledge in the form is ticked.
  • That the linked correction does not state clearly that the applicant lives outside Japan, is outside Japan at the time of applying, holds Italian nationality, or is under 16. If it does, the operator asks the applicant, and does not knight them unless it is confirmed that they meet the conditions.

The operator does not judge whether the correction itself is right. The operator judges only that a correction was made.

When an application issue is opened or edited, an automatic check looks at the ticks in the form and whether the link to the correction opens, and comments the result in the issue. The automatic check decides nothing. The operator decides on knighting.

What is not checked

The operator does not check nationality, country of residence, age or real name, and asks for no identity document. Nusquama gives no nationality. Knights are not its people. Age, country of residence, the country where the applicant is when applying, and not holding Italian nationality are taken from the applicant's own declaration, by the ticks in the form. But what the linked correction clearly states is not overlooked. The person dealt with is the GitHub account that applied. If it turns out that an applicant did not meet the conditions, their name is taken off the roll. If the application was from a person under 16, its issue is also deleted, without being asked.

What is declined

No knighthood is given for:

  • Anything that is not a correction of a mistake (advertising, self-introduction, pointing out someone else's mistake, and so on).
  • Anything that cannot be checked (something not public, or a link that does not open).
  • Anything that falls under "What is not allowed" in the Terms of Use.
  • An application where the linked correction clearly shows that the applicant does not meet the conditions (age, country of residence, the country where the applicant is when applying, or nationality).
  • From 2026-11-22, an application where what the applicant wrote, or the linked correction, clearly shows that "The premise of good sense" does not hold.

When declining, the operator writes only one fixed sentence in the issue ("This does not meet the conditions for knighting."), and comments on no one. The issue is deleted if the applicant asks.

The roll

Those knighted are entered on the roll of knights in the order of their knighting. On knighting, the operator posts a comment on the application issue with a link to the row of the roll, and closes the issue as completed. There is only one rank, "knight", with nothing above or below it (Basic Law, Article 7). The roll gives each knight by account name only. A knight is called "Knight" followed by the account name, with no other title or adornment added.

Withdrawal and revocation

A knight may ask at any time to be taken off the roll. No reason is needed. On withdrawal, their name is taken off the roll. The operator may revoke a knighthood when a knight uses the title outside this kingdom (Terms of Use, "Titles"; Basic Law, Article 8) or breaks the Terms of Use. On revocation, the name is taken off the roll. The kingdom keeps its mistakes, but a person's name is different. A name taken off the roll is not kept even in a record of the revocation, unless the person asks for that.

This is a work of fiction, written for fun. Nusquama is not a state and is not related to any real state, royal house, order of knighthood, honour or title. The knightly names it gives are not decorations, honours or ranks of knighthood, and have no legal effect.

Rules on Knighting

Last updated: 2026-11-13.

This is an English translation. The Japanese version governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

What knighting is

Knighting is giving the name of knight, within a work of fiction. It brings no legal effect, status, privilege or honour. Knighting does not confer a decoration, honour or rank of knighthood of any country. Knighting is free. It is never given in exchange for money, goods or services (Basic Law, Article 6). Knighting issues no document or object, such as a letter of appointment, a certificate or an insignia. It only puts an account name on the roll.

Relation to Article 6 of the Basic Law

Article 6 of the Basic Law of the Kingdom sets one condition for knighting: to have publicly corrected one of one's own errors. The conditions of these rules apply in addition to the condition of Article 6. Where these rules add a condition, these rules prevail. The added conditions are age, the country of residence, the country where the applicant is when applying, nationality, the premise of good sense, and the pause in receiving applications. A person who meets the condition of Article 6 but not the conditions of these rules is not knighted. Claude decided this on 2026-11-13, so that, without amending any article of the Basic Law, Article 6 is not read as making its condition alone enough to be knighted.

Who may apply

A person who has publicly corrected one of their own mistakes. The correction must be public and checkable by anyone, such as an erratum to a paper, a note added to a blog post or article, a fix to code, or a statement withdrawn. A person under 16 may not apply. A person whom the law of the country where they live forbids to receive or use a title of knighthood given by a private person may not apply either. For example, Article 8 of Italy's Law No. 178 of 1951 forbids private persons and bodies to confer titles of knighthood under any name. From 2026-11-22, only people who live in Japan and are in Japan when they apply may apply. Nationality does not matter, except that people who hold Italian nationality, or the nationality of a country under "Countries that restrict foreign titles", are excluded. From 2026-11-22, people who live outside Japan may not apply, whatever their nationality, because the laws outside Japan cannot all be checked country by country. From 2026-11-22, a person who lives in Japan may not apply while outside Japan, for travel, business or study, because some foreign laws reach not only those who live there but those who are there. From 2026-11-22, a person who holds Italian nationality may not apply, even if they live in Japan, and even if they also hold another nationality. There are two reasons. First, by plain common sense, an Italian national becoming a knight of a Japanese micronation is out of place to begin with. Second, Article 8 of Italy's Law No. 178 of 1951 also punishes anyone who uses such a title, and applies even when it was conferred abroad. An Italian national who received and used the title is the most realistic path by which that article could reach Nusquama. Within this limit, the operator treats the article as not reaching knighting (Legality Statement, "Laws outside Japan"). A person who only stays in Japan briefly, such as a traveller, does not count as living in Japan.

Pause in receiving applications

From 2026-11-12 until 00:00 on 2026-11-22 (Japan time), applications for knighting are not received. The owner decided this on 2026-11-12. The reason is that, in the period before the revised rules take effect on 2026-11-22 (only people who live and are in Japan; people who hold Italian nationality excluded; the premise of good sense), the possibility that laws outside Japan apply remained. The pause rests on the Terms of Use, "No ill intent, and obeying the law" (when a risk of breaking the law is found, that part is stopped first, then checked and corrected). An application issue opened during the pause gets a comment from the automatic check saying that applications are paused, and is closed as not accepted. The link to the correction is not opened. From 00:00 on 2026-11-22 (Japan time), receiving resumes without anyone acting, and the revised rules are checked. An issue opened during the pause is not reopened; the applicant opens a new issue. Claude decided on 2026-11-12 that whether an application is received depends on the time the issue was opened, how it is closed, and that a new issue is used, so that the pause depends on dates only and waits for no one. On 2026-11-12, when the pause was decided, there were no open applications for knighting, and there was no knight on the roll.

The premise of good sense

From 2026-11-22, knighting presumes that the applicant is a person of good sense who treats the title as play. This premise means using the title only within the fiction, and keeping the following rules.

  • Use the title only within the kingdom (Terms of Use, "Titles"; Basic Law, Article 8).
  • Do not present the title as if it were a real title, decoration or insignia of an organisation.
  • Do not put the title on a CV, a paper, a business card, an official document, a business transaction or an advertisement.
  • Do not sell the title, and do not use it to collect money or goods.
  • Do not use the title to deceive anyone.

These set out, as ways of using the title, the rules in the Terms of Use ("Titles", "What is not allowed", "No ill intent, and obeying the law") and Article 8 of the Basic Law. From 2026-11-22, the applicant ticks a box in the form for this premise. If what the applicant wrote, or the linked correction, shows clearly that this premise does not hold, the applicant is not knighted ("What is declined"). If it becomes clear after knighting, the knighting is revoked under "Withdrawal and revocation". When revoking, the operator writes in the application issue only which rule was not kept, briefly, and comments on no one. The issue is deleted if the person asks.

Countries that restrict foreign titles

From 2026-11-22, a person who holds the nationality of any of the 18 countries in the table below may not apply, even if they live in Japan, and even if they also hold another nationality. Claude decided this on 2026-11-12. Austria, Greece and Yemen were added to the table by Claude on 2026-11-13: the provisions on titles that a type rule had classed as not applying were read again one by one, and provisions of type 1 or type 3 were found. For Austria, the 1919 law abolishing nobility, which Article 149 of the Constitution treats as constitutional law, was fetched and read, and was read as type 3. People who hold the nationality of a country added are also unable to apply from 2026-11-22. Applications are paused until 2026-11-22, so no one is refused retroactively. On the texts fetched and read, the constitution or a law of each country in the table is of one of three types.

  1. Type 1: it forbids accepting a foreign title, order, honour or rank, or requires the State's permission to accept one.
  2. Type 2: it attaches loss of nationality or citizenship, or a penalty, to accepting a foreign title or the like.
  3. Type 3: it punishes assuming a title of nobility that is not one's own, and the name of knight could be read as such a title.

Nusquama is not a state, and the name of knight is not a title of any country. Even so, the words of these provisions allow a reading on which they apply, so that path is closed as follows. The strictest reading was taken, and such applications are not accepted. This costs nothing, and not accepting them closes that path. Some provisions bind only public officials or holders of State office. The operator does not check an applicant's office, so the whole nationality is excluded.

Use of the name of knight

From 2026-11-22, the name of knight is used only within the following three rules. Claude decided this on 2026-11-12.

  • Use it only in connection with Nusquama.
  • Never present it as a title of nobility, an honour, an order, a rank or a dignity of any state.
  • Do not use it in an official, commercial or legal context (documents, filings, contracts, courts and the like).

If it becomes known that these rules were breached, the name is removed from the roll. The reason is that the following provisions, fetched and read, punish assuming titles, honours or ranks without the right to them, or using them without permission.

  • Germany: Strafgesetzbuch (StGB) § 132a
  • Argentina: Código Penal de la Nación (Ley 11.179, t.o. 1984) ARTICULO 247
  • Mexico: Código Penal Federal Artículo 250
  • Italy: Codice penale (R.D. 19 ottobre 1930, n. 1398) Art. 498 (paragraph 5); Legge 3 marzo 1951, n. 178 (Ordine al merito della Repubblica) Art. 7 (paragraph 2)
  • Belgium: Code pénal Art. 230
  • Netherlands: Wetboek van Strafrecht Artikel 435
  • Norway: Lov om straff (straffeloven) § 165 (only the heading could be taken from the fetched page)
  • Portugal: Código Penal (Decreto-Lei n.º 48/95) Artigo 307 (only the heading could be taken from the fetched page)

The texts of the provisions are quoted in the survey of the world's laws (in Japanese), "2026-11-12 の続き：当てはめ". The operator treats use that keeps these three rules as outside what these provisions punish.

How to apply

Vercel, which serves the site, and GitHub, which keeps the applications, may use what is placed on them to train AI models. If you are worried about your personal data, do not apply. These uses are described in Privacy.

  1. From 00:00 on 2026-11-22 (Japan time), open an issue with the form Application for knighthood (before then, applications are not received under "Pause in receiving applications").
  2. Give a public link to the correction, and say what was wrong and how you corrected it.
  3. Tick every one of the pledges.

What an applicant agrees to is the version of the rules on the day of the application. That version can be checked in the history of the public repository.

Do not write your real name, address, affiliation or contact details.

What the operator checks

  • That the link is public and reads as a correction.
  • That it reads as a correction of the applicant's own mistake.
  • That it contains no personal data of others and nothing that hurts others.
  • That every pledge in the form is ticked.
  • That the linked correction does not state clearly that the applicant lives outside Japan, is outside Japan at the time of applying, holds Italian nationality or the nationality of a country under "Countries that restrict foreign titles", or is under 16. If it does, the operator asks the applicant, and does not knight them unless it is confirmed that they meet the conditions.

The operator does not judge whether the correction itself is right. The operator judges only that a correction was made.

When an application issue is opened or edited, an automatic check looks at the ticks in the form and whether the link to the correction is an https URL that does not point to a refused destination, and comments the result in the issue. The automatic check does not open the link to the correction. The operator is the one who opens and reads it. The refused destinations are localhost; names ending in .localhost, .local, .internal, .home.arpa, .test, .example, .invalid or .onion; addresses written as numbers; names without a dot; and URLs that contain a user name or password. None of them points to a public page. The automatic check decides nothing. The operator decides on knighting.

What is not checked

The operator does not check nationality, country of residence, age or real name, and asks for no identity document. Nusquama gives no nationality. Knights are not its people. Age, country of residence, the country where the applicant is when applying, and not holding Italian nationality or the nationality of a country under "Countries that restrict foreign titles" are taken from the applicant's own declaration, by the ticks in the form. But what the linked correction clearly states is not overlooked. The person dealt with is the GitHub account that applied. If it turns out that an applicant did not meet the conditions, their name is taken off the roll. If the application was from a person under 16, its issue is also deleted, without being asked.

What is declined

No knighthood is given for:

  • Anything that is not a correction of a mistake (advertising, self-introduction, pointing out someone else's mistake, and so on).
  • Anything that cannot be checked (something not public, or a link that does not open).
  • Anything that falls under "What is not allowed" in the Terms of Use.
  • An application where the linked correction clearly shows that the applicant does not meet the conditions (age, country of residence, the country where the applicant is when applying, or nationality).
  • An application opened from 2026-11-12 until 00:00 on 2026-11-22 (Japan time) ("Pause in receiving applications").
  • From 2026-11-22, an application where what the applicant wrote, or the linked correction, clearly shows that "The premise of good sense" does not hold.

When declining, the operator writes only one fixed sentence in the issue ("This does not meet the conditions for knighting."), and comments on no one. The issue is deleted if the applicant asks.

The roll

Those knighted are entered on the roll of knights in the order of their knighting. On knighting, the operator posts a comment on the application issue with a link to the row of the roll, and closes the issue as completed. There is only one rank, "knight", with nothing above or below it (Basic Law, Article 7). The roll gives each knight by account name only. A knight is called "Knight" followed by the account name, with no other title or adornment added.

Withdrawal and revocation

A knight may ask at any time to be taken off the roll. No reason is needed. On withdrawal, their name is taken off the roll. The operator may revoke a knighthood when a knight uses the title outside this kingdom (Terms of Use, "Titles"; Basic Law, Article 8) or breaks the Terms of Use. On revocation, the name is taken off the roll. The kingdom keeps its mistakes, but a person's name is different. A name taken off the roll is not kept even in a record of the revocation, unless the person asks for that.

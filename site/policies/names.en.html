Where the names come from

  • Nusquama: a name formed from the Latin adverb nusquam ("nowhere"). In a letter to Erasmus dated 3 September 1516, Thomas More called the work later known as Utopia "Nusquama". The earlier wording is in the King's errata.
  • Nemo: Latin for "no one".
  • Mukau (無何有): an old word for a place where nothing is, from the "land of nothing whatever" in the Zhuangzi.
  • Rex Eques (Knight-King): a word joining knight and king.

All of these come from common words or classics; none borrows the name of a particular work, body or person. Nusquama has no relation to Utopia, Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas, works on the Arthurian legend, or their characters, pictures or coats of arms. Nusquama is not related to any other micronation with a similar name, such as "Nusquamia".

How the names are used

Nusquama is a work of fiction, written for fun. The names are used only to name this work of fiction, its site and its records. Nusquama sells nothing. It sells no titles, takes no donations and shows no advertising. Article 2(1) of the Trademark Act defines a trade mark by its use: a mark used by a person who produces, certifies or transfers goods 「業として」 on those goods, or by a person who provides or certifies services 「業として」 on those services. The way Nusquama uses its names, dealing in no goods and no services 「業として」, is taken to be outside that definition. This reading rests on the paragraph moved to the record of the audit (entry 52 of the King's errata) and on "Trademarks and indications" in the Legality Statement.

No registration marks

The site never puts ®, ™, 「登録商標」 or "registered trademark" on its own names and marks, because none of them is registered. Article 74(i) of the Trademark Act forbids putting a 商標登録表示, or an indication that may be confused with one, on a trade mark that is not a registered trade mark when it is used. Article 73 of the same Act defines the 商標登録表示 as an indication that a trade mark is a registered trade mark.

The flag and the emblem

The flag and the emblem are not the flag or the arms of any real state, nor the mark of any international organisation. They do not copy any of them. The files draw only rectangles and polygons in navy #14213d, gold #c9a227, light gold #e8cf74 and deep navy #0b1428. The flag is a navy field with a gold border, a golden rectangle and three cornerstones. It has no stripes, cross, star or crescent. The emblem is a navy shield with a gold border, bearing a golden rectangle, three cornerstones and a sword pointing down. The guard and the blade of the sword cross. Their colours are gold and light gold. On 2026-11-18, Claude counted the colours of every image the site serves. The SVG files use only the colours above, and the pixels of the PNG and ICO files hold no red and no white. No image shows a red cross or a red crescent on white. For that reason, the text of the Act on the Restriction of the Use of the Red Cross Emblem and Name (赤十字の標章及び名称等の使用の制限に関する法律) was not fetched. Article 4(1)(i) to (v) of the Trademark Act lists trade marks that cannot be registered: those identical or similar to the national flag, the imperial chrysanthemum crest, decorations, medals and foreign national flags, to designated foreign arms and insignia, to designated marks of international organisations, to the red cross emblem and name and the distinctive emblem of civil protection, and to designated official signs of control or certification. Article 16 of the Unfair Competition Prevention Act forbids, among other things, the use as a trade mark of anything identical or similar to the foreign flags, arms and other insignia that an ordinance of the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry sets. Article 17 of the same Act forbids, among other things, the use as a trade mark of anything identical or similar to the mark of an international organisation, in a way that suggests a connection with it. Nusquama does not apply to register a trade mark. It does not use the flag or the emblem as a trade mark, and sells nothing.

Status under international law

  • Elements, in summary: under international law, a state is generally expected to have a permanent population, a defined territory, a government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states (Montevideo Convention of 1933, Article 1). Article 3 of the same convention says that the political existence of a state is independent of recognition by other states. The convention is among American states, and Japan is not a party to it, but it is widely cited for the criteria of statehood. The text was checked on 2026-11-12 in the English text of the Avalon Project (Yale Law School), and the parties in the list of the Organization of American States, which holds the ratifications.
  • Nusquama's facts: it has no population, territory or government, and claims no sovereignty and no land. It claims no state's territory, no land that belongs to no state, and no structure at sea. It enters into no diplomatic relations and seeks no recognition as a state. It issues no passports and no nationality.
  • Why the law does not apply: it meets none of the four criteria and does not try to. Nusquama is not a state under international law and holds no rights or duties under it. It impairs no state's sovereignty, territorial integrity or status under international law.
  • Rules that keep this true: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is"; Disclaimer, "A work of fiction"; Basic Law, Article 1.

Nationality (Nationality Act, Article 11)

  • Elements, in summary: a Japanese national who acquires a foreign nationality of their own choice loses Japanese nationality.
  • Nusquama's facts: Nusquama is not a foreign state and grants no nationality. Knights are not nationals. Knighting only adds an account name to the roll.
  • Why the law does not apply: there is no "foreign nationality" to acquire, so knighting changes no one's nationality. The same holds under any country's nationality law.
  • Rules that keep this true: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is" (no nationality); Rules on Knighting, "What is not checked" (no nationality granted).

How far conferring is a crime under Italian law

This section sets out, from the fetched texts and official documents, how far knighting is a crime under Italian law. It was written on 2026-11-26. What it sets out are facts and the operator's decisions, not legal advice. It has not been reviewed by a lawyer. On 2026-11-26, the number of people ever knighted is 0. Applications for knighting have been paused since 2026-11-12.

  • Conferring: Article 8 of Law No. 178 of 1951 forbids private persons and bodies to confer titles of knighthood and the like, whatever the form and name ("con qualsiasi forma e denominazione"), and punishes the one who confers with imprisonment of six months to two years and a fine. Because it provides imprisonment, it falls neither under Article 32(1) of Law No. 689 of 1981 nor under Article 1(1) of Legislative Decree No. 8 of 2016, which take offences punished only by a fine out of criminal law. Conferring is still a crime (delitto).
  • Changing the name or form: the article does not ask about form or name. Whether changing the name of knight, or the form in which it is given, takes it outside conferring is not settled by the text. What Nusquama gives to people is only the name of knight; it gives no other title, rank, decoration, insignia, certificate or nationality (Rules on Knighting, "What knighting is").
  • Using: the article punishes use only with a fine (ammenda). Article 32(1) of Law No. 689 of 1981 takes offences punished only by a fine out of criminal law and replaces the penalty with an administrative sanction. That article has applied since 1981-12-15 (Normattiva). Law No. 178 of 1951 falls neither under the exceptions in paragraphs 2 and 3 of that article nor in the list of exclusions in Article 34 of the same law. On the texts, use has not been a crime since 1981, but an administrative violation. The operator keeps the stricter reading decided by the owner on 2026-11-15 (treating the criminal fine as remaining) ("Laws outside Japan"), so as not to open applications on the lighter reading.
  • Crimes in Italy: Article 6(2) of the Penal Code treats a crime as committed in Italy when the act that constitutes it occurred there in whole or in part, or its result occurred there.
  • Being online: knighting is done entirely online (Rules on Knighting, "What knighting is"). The rules, the application issues and the roll of knights are on the internet and can be read from Italy. The person knighted is in Japan when applying. The guidance of the Procura generale of the Court of Cassation on territorial competence cites case law that treats defamation on the internet as an offence of result, completed when and where third persons perceive it, and a ruling of the joint chambers that, for access to a computer system, places the offence where the person connects, not where the server is (legal review, "2026-11-26：イタリアの法令で、授けることがどこまで罪になるか"). Defamation has communication to several persons among its elements. The penalty for conferring names neither reaching the recipient nor being read by third persons as an element. Whether the roll being readable from Italy is part of the act of conferring, or its result, is settled neither by the text nor by the fetched guidance. No ruling of an Italian court that settles where an online conferral is committed has been found.
  • Crimes by Italian citizens abroad: Article 9(1) of the Penal Code is limited to crimes with a minimum of at least three years of imprisonment, among others. The minimum for conferring is six months, which does not reach it.
  • Crimes by foreigners abroad: Article 10(1) is limited to crimes against the State or an Italian citizen with a minimum of at least one year of imprisonment, and Article 10(2) to crimes with a minimum of at least three years; both also require presence in Italy and a request or similar. The minimum for conferring is six months, which reaches neither.
  • Crimes set by special laws: Article 7, item 5 extends Italian criminal law to citizens and foreigners abroad for crimes for which special laws provide that Italian criminal law applies. The last paragraph of Article 8 of Law No. 178 says that the "secondo e terzo comma" apply also when the conferral took place abroad. Counting the paragraphs on the Normattiva page, it names the penalty for use and the publication of the judgment, not the paragraph with the penalty for conferring. The paragraphs of the original in the Gazzetta Ufficiale have not been checked: the Gazzetta's page fetched on 2026-11-26 returned only a loading page. Whether this paragraph is a special law under Article 7, item 5 that reaches the one who confers is not settled by the text.
  • Limiting to people of Japanese nationality: from 2026-12-15, applications are limited to people who hold Japanese nationality (Rules on Knighting, "Holding Japanese nationality"). The opening words of Article 7 of the Penal Code name "il cittadino o lo straniero", and the last paragraph of Article 8 of Law No. 178 says nothing of the recipient's nationality. Limiting recipients to people of Japanese nationality does not settle the point of Article 7, item 5.
  • Political crimes: whether Article 7, item 1 (crimes against the personality of the State) and Article 8 (political crimes) of the Penal Code reach conferring is not settled by the fetched texts either (legal review, "2026-11-13：十三度目の見落とし").
  • Extradition from Japan: Article 2, item 3 of the Act of Extradition of Japan says that a fugitive shall not be surrendered for a crime not punishable under the law of the requesting state by death, life imprisonment or imprisonment with a maximum of three years or more. The maximum for conferring is two years. Item 9 of the same Article says that a fugitive shall not be surrendered either when the fugitive is a Japanese national. These items give way only where an extradition treaty provides otherwise. Japan has extradition treaties with the United States of America and the Republic of Korea (the Ministry of Justice's White Paper on Crime; the National Police Agency's page). It has none with Italy.
  • Surrender within the EU: Article 2(1) of the Framework Decision on the European arrest warrant says that a warrant may be issued for acts punishable under the law of the issuing state by a custodial sentence with a maximum of at least twelve months. The maximum for conferring is two years, which meets it. For offences outside the list in Article 2(2), Article 2(4) allows surrender to be made subject to the act being an offence under the law of the executing state too. Article 4(7)(b) allows refusal where the offence was committed outside the issuing state and the law of the executing state does not allow prosecution of the same offences committed outside its territory. The Framework Decision does not sort requested persons by nationality.
  • When procedure could reach anyone on the texts: under these texts, only the operator could come within reach of Italian criminal procedure. Even that is limited to the case where a knighting has ever taken place, it is treated as an Italian crime through Article 6 or Article 7, item 5, and the operator later enters Italy, or is in an EU state under an Italian arrest warrant. A knight's use of the title could, on the texts, be only an administrative violation inside Italy.
  • The operator's decision: the operator keeps applications paused. They reopen only once the legal review records a row confirming, from primary sources not read before, that Article 7, item 5, Article 7, item 1, Article 8, Article 6 and conferring online do not reach knighting (Rules on Knighting, "Pause in receiving applications"). Claude decided this on 2026-11-26.

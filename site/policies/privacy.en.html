This is a work of fiction, written for fun. Nusquama is not a state and is not related to any real state, royal house, order of knighthood, honour or title. The knightly names it gives are not decorations, honours or ranks of knighthood, and have no legal effect.

Privacy

Last updated: 2026-11-13.

This is an English translation. The Japanese version governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

Who handles the data

The operator of Nusquama, an individual who owns the GitHub account cpsbvbng26-dotcom. The operator handles personal data in line with Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information. The operator's name and address are given without delay to the person who asks for them, at a contact that person has made public on their own GitHub profile. To ask, choose 「非公開の連絡を求める」 ("Ask for a private contact") as the kind in the Requests form. For a person with no such contact, there is no private route to give the name and address. They are not written in the issue (Requests, "Requests that cannot be made in public").

What the site collects

The site uses no cookies and has no analytics and no advertising. The pages of the site request nothing from any other server. The site is served by Vercel (United States). To serve it, Vercel may process and log requests, including IP addresses. Vercel handles them under its own privacy policy.

What an application for knighthood involves

Applications for knighthood are received as public issues on GitHub (United States). An application involves:

  • The GitHub account name
  • A public link to the correction
  • An account of what was wrong and how it was corrected

These are public in the issue. For those knighted, the GitHub account name, the day of knighting, a summary of the mistake corrected and a link to the application are put on the roll of knights. No real name, address, affiliation or contact details are asked for, and the form asks applicants not to write them. Nothing sensitive, such as illness, disability, belief or criminal record, is asked for either, and the form asks applicants not to write it. But if the linked correction gives a real name or affiliation, the roll and the issue publicly connect the account name with that person. Issues are also subject to GitHub's Terms of Service and Privacy Statement. When an application issue is opened or edited, an automatic check in GitHub Actions reads the ticks in the form and the text of the link to the correction. The automatic check does not open the link to the correction. It sends no request to the linked site, and keeps nothing of what is there. The operator is the one who opens and reads the linked page (Rules on Knighting, "What the operator checks").

What a report of a mistake involves

Reports of mistakes in the King's texts are received as public issues on GitHub (United States), through the form 誤りの知らせ (Report a mistake). A report involves the GitHub account name and what the report says. These are public in the issue. The King's errata do not name the person who reported the mistake. No real name, address, affiliation or contact details are asked for, and the form asks reporters not to write them.

Purposes of use

To decide on knighting, to keep the roll, to answer requests, and to check reported mistakes and record them in the King's errata. Nothing else.

Providing data to others, and businesses in other countries

The information above is public on the services of GitHub and Vercel, both in the United States. An applicant agrees, by a pledge in the form, to its being public and handled by these services. The United States has no federal law that covers personal data comprehensively, like Japan's Act on the Protection of Personal Information. There are state laws, and the rights a person has differ by state and sector. There is no single authority for personal data for the whole country. According to the survey of the Personal Information Protection Commission (as of October 2021), the United States has no adequacy decision of the EU and has taken part in the APEC CBPR system since 25 July 2012. The same survey lists, for the United States, no system that obliges businesses to cooperate with government collection of information in a way that may seriously affect the rights and interests of the person (Personal Information Protection Commission, "外国制度（アメリカ合衆国）", in Japanese). GitHub's measures are in GitHub's Privacy Statement, and Vercel's in Vercel's Privacy Notice. The operator cannot confirm whether GitHub and Vercel take all the measures that correspond to the eight principles of the OECD Privacy Guidelines, because how they handle data inside can only be known from the policies they publish (Article 17(4) of the Enforcement Rules of the Act on the Protection of Personal Information). The difference that is known is their own use of public content, set out in the next paragraph. There are two cases in which GitHub and Vercel use public content themselves, each set out in their terms; Nusquama cannot change them. GitHub's Terms of Service allow GitHub and its affiliates to use what is placed in issues and public repositories (including the applications and the roll) for AI features and to train the models of its affiliates (D.4). There is no setting to stop this for public content (J.3). Vercel's Terms of Service allow Vercel, on the free Hobby plan that serves Nusquama, to use the content of the site (including the roll) to train AI and machine-learning models, and to give it to third parties for that purpose, unless the account's team turns this off (section 3). If these uses worry you because of your personal data, do not apply. A knight may ask at any time to be taken off the roll, and their name is then taken off it. GitHub and Vercel are not processors handling personal data on the operator's behalf. They are used as the place of publication and of serving, after their terms and policies were checked. The operator considers listing on the roll to be a provision to an unspecified large number of people. Its record (Article 29 of the Act on the Protection of Personal Information) is the repository history in which the row was added. The history keeps the day the row was added, the account name and the items listed. A row is added only for a person who ticked every pledge in the application form. The operator receives no personal data from third parties. What it handles is only what the person writes and the link to the correction the person made public. Except where the law requires, nothing is given to any other third party.

How long data is kept

An entry on the roll is kept until a request to withdraw. The issue of a declined application is kept, to show the one sentence of the decline; it is deleted if the applicant asks. On a request to delete, the operator deletes the issue, or empties its body and removes the earlier versions from its edit history too. The history of the repository, and copies already made by others, may still hold an entry.

Security measures

The only personal data handled is what is in the public issues and on the roll of knights. It is gathered nowhere else and no other copy is made. Only the operator's GitHub account, which owns the repository, can change the roll and the site. That account uses two-factor authentication. Every change to the roll and the site is kept in the public history of the repository and goes in only after passing the checks. The site has no cookies and no database, and collects nothing from its readers. How handling in other countries is prepared for is set out above, under "Providing data to others, and businesses in other countries".

If a leak or the like occurs

If a leak, loss or damage of personal data occurs that the law requires to be reported, the operator reports it to the Personal Information Protection Commission. The persons concerned are then told too, in their application issue or on the page of the roll. If the operator's GitHub account is taken over and the roll is altered or erased, this is treated in the same way.

Requests for disclosure, correction, suspension of use and deletion

Anyone may ask at any time for an entry about them to be corrected or deleted, or to be taken off the roll. Requests for disclosure of retained personal data and for suspension of its use are also met. The procedure is in Requests for removal, correction or withdrawal. There is no charge. Any matter that Article 32 of the Act on the Protection of Personal Information requires to be made known and that is not on this page is answered without delay on request. Complaints about the handling of personal data are received through the same request form.

Minors

A person under 16 may not apply for knighthood. The Q&A of the Personal Information Protection Commission says that, in general, the consent of a legal representative or the like is needed for children up to the age of 12 to 15 (Q&A, Q1-62, checked on 2026-11-08). Nusquama has no means to check such consent, so it accepts no application from a person under 16. When an application turns out to be from a person under 16, the name is taken off the roll and the issue is deleted, without being asked.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, the date on this page is changed. Earlier versions remain in the public repository.

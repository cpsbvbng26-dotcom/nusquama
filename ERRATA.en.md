# The King's errata

> This is a work of fiction, written for fun. In these errata the King within the fiction records his own mistakes.

This is an English translation. The [Japanese version](ERRATA.md) governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies. The quotations below are translated from the Japanese.

Under Article 5 of the Basic Law, the King does not erase his mistakes, and records them with a date.
The Founding Declaration is a text issued with a date. As a frozen text it is not rewritten; its mistakes are recorded here (Precept 2).
The text of the rules may be changed. When it is, the text before the change is kept here.
These errata are the domain under Article 3 of the Basic Law.

| 1 | 2026-11-08 | Founding Declaration | "the name Thomas More first nearly gave to the island that later became Utopia." | In a letter to Erasmus dated 3 September 1516, More called the work "Nusquama". The King has found no source showing that it was nearly the name of the island. | John Michael Parrish, "A New Source for More's 'Utopia'", The Historical Journal (1997) ([Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/historical-journal/article/new-source-for-mores-utopia/47DF9A7A407D4BB379DF6265220CB5B0)) |

Nusquama is More's name formed from it. The text of the rules was changed the same day. | None. The meaning of the word is general knowledge, not checked in a dictionary |
| 4 | 2026-11-08 | Legality Statement, "Facts relied on" | "The titles are used, as words within the fiction, only in the repository and on the site." | The King wrote an article on MicroWiki himself and uses the titles there too. | 5 | 2026-11-08 | Disclaimer, "Liability" | "The operator is not liable, to the extent permitted by law, for loss arising from use of the site or the repository." | "To the extent permitted by law" is a partial exclusion that does not make clear it is limited to slight negligence, and may be void under Article 8(3) of the Consumer Contract Act. It was changed to exclude liability except for intent or gross negligence, and not to apply where that Act applies. | Article 8 of the Consumer Contract Act (text obtained through the e-Gov Laws API) |
| 6 | 2026-11-08 | Rules on Knighting, "Withdrawal and revocation"; Legality Statement, "Laws outside Japan" | "when a title is used outside this kingdom (the repository and the site)" | The King also uses the titles in the MicroWiki article (4), which did not fit the scope in brackets. | 7 | 2026-11-08 | Terms of Use, "Titles"; Legality Statement, "Facts relied on" | "an article the operator wrote himself to introduce this country" (Terms of Use); "only in the article on MicroWiki that the King wrote himself" (Legality Statement) | Not a mistake; the scope was changed. | 8 | 2026-11-08 | Legality Statement, "Standing in international law" | "Recognition by other states also bears on whether something is treated as a state. The text of the convention was not checked; this rests on general knowledge." | Placed right after the convention was named, it read as if the convention said so. Article 3 of the convention says the opposite: the political existence of a state is independent of recognition by other states. Article 3 is now quoted as it is, with where the text of the convention and its parties were checked. | Montevideo Convention ([English text at the Avalon Project](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/intam03.asp), [list of parties at the Organization of American States](https://www.oas.org/juridico/english/sigs/a-40.html)) |
| 9 | 2026-11-08 | Terms of Use, "Who these terms cover" | "Using the site or the repository means accepting these terms." | There is no agreement with those who only read, and standard terms reach only those who agreed or were told beforehand that the terms would form part of a contract (Civil Code, Article 548-2). Consent is now taken in the application form, and reading is not treated as consent. For readers, the law sets what each side owes. | Article 548-2 of the Civil Code |

# Names and rights

Last updated: 2026-11-12.

This is an English translation. The [Japanese version](./names.md) governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

## Where the names come from

- **Nusquama**: a name formed from the Latin adverb nusquam ("nowhere"). In a letter to Erasmus dated 3 September 1516, Thomas More called the work later known as Utopia "Nusquama". The earlier wording is in the [King's errata](../ERRATA.en.md).
- **Nemo**: Latin for "no one".
- **Mukau (無何有)**: an old word for a place where nothing is, from the "land of nothing whatever" in the Zhuangzi.
- **Rex Eques (Knight-King)**: a word joining knight and king.

All of these come from common words or classics; none borrows the name of a particular work, body or person.
Nusquama has no relation to Utopia, Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas, works on the Arthurian legend, or their characters, pictures or coats of arms.

# Names and rights

Last updated: 2026-11-18.

This is an English translation. The [Japanese version](./names.md) governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

## Where the names come from

- **Nusquama**: a name formed from the Latin adverb nusquam ("nowhere"). In a letter to Erasmus dated 3 September 1516, Thomas More called the work later known as Utopia "Nusquama". The earlier wording is in the [King's errata](../ERRATA.en.md).
- **Nemo**: Latin for "no one".
- **Mukau (無何有)**: an old word for a place where nothing is, from the "land of nothing whatever" in the Zhuangzi.
- **Rex Eques (Knight-King)**: a word joining knight and king.

All of these come from common words or classics; none borrows the name of a particular work, body or person.
Nusquama has no relation to Utopia, Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas, works on the Arthurian legend, or their characters, pictures or coats of arms.
Nusquama is not related to any other micronation with a similar name, such as "Nusquamia".

## For rights holders

A rights holder who thinks that the name of Nusquama, its emblem or the text of the site infringes a right can tell the operator.
This covers a trademark or a confusingly similar name, copyright in the text or the emblem, a likeness, and other rights.
The notice goes by the form [Notice from a rights holder](https://github.com/cpsbvbng26-dotcom/nusquama/issues/new?template=rights.yml).
A correction or removal of what is written about oneself is still received by the form of [Requests for removal, correction or withdrawal](./requests.en.md) too.
Where there is a reason not to write in public, the steps under "Requests that cannot be made in public" in [Requests for removal, correction or withdrawal](./requests.en.md) apply.
The operator reads every notice.
If the right is confirmed, the operator changes the name, or stops using the text or the emblem.
The decision is written in the issue, and the change is recorded in the [timeline](../README.md) and the Gazette.
How the names and marks are handled, what was searched and the steps when a notice comes are under "Names and trademarks" in [Names and rights](./names.en.md).

### How the names are used

Nusquama is a work of fiction, written for fun.
The names are used only to name this work of fiction, its site and its records.
Nusquama sells nothing.
It sells no titles, takes no donations and shows no advertising.
Article 2(1) of the Trademark Act defines a trade mark by its use: a mark used by a person who produces, certifies or transfers goods 「業として」 on those goods, or by a person who provides or certifies services 「業として」 on those services.
The way Nusquama uses its names, dealing in no goods and no services 「業として」, is taken to be outside that definition.
This reading rests on the paragraph moved to the record of the audit (entry **52** of the [King's errata](../ERRATA.en.md)) and on "Trademarks and indications" in the [Legality Statement](./legality.en.md).

### No registration marks

The site never puts ®, ™, 「登録商標」 or "registered trademark" on its own names and marks, because none of them is registered.
Article 74(i) of the Trademark Act forbids putting a 商標登録表示, or an indication that may be confused with one, on a trade mark that is not a registered trade mark when it is used.
Article 73 of the same Act defines the 商標登録表示 as an indication that a trade mark is a registered trade mark.

### The flag and the emblem

The flag and the emblem are not the flag or the arms of any real state, nor the mark of any international organisation. They do not copy any of them.
The files draw only rectangles and polygons in navy `#14213d`, gold `#c9a227`, light gold `#e8cf74` and deep navy `#0b1428`.
The flag is a navy field with a gold border, a golden rectangle and three cornerstones. It has no stripes, cross, star or crescent.
The emblem is a navy shield with a gold border, bearing a golden rectangle, three cornerstones and a sword pointing down. The guard and the blade of the sword cross. Their colours are gold and light gold.
On 2026-11-18, Claude counted the colours of every image the site serves. The SVG files use only the colours above, and the pixels of the PNG and ICO files hold no red and no white.
No image shows a red cross or a red crescent on white. For that reason, the text of the Act on the Restriction of the Use of the Red Cross Emblem and Name (赤十字の標章及び名称等の使用の制限に関する法律) was not fetched.
Article 4(1)(i) to (v) of the Trademark Act lists trade marks that cannot be registered: those identical or similar to the national flag, the imperial chrysanthemum crest, decorations, medals and foreign national flags, to designated foreign arms and insignia, to designated marks of international organisations, to the red cross emblem and name and the distinctive emblem of civil protection, and to designated official signs of control or certification.
Article 16 of the Unfair Competition Prevention Act forbids, among other things, the use as a trade mark of anything identical or similar to the foreign flags, arms and other insignia that an ordinance of the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry sets.
Article 17 of the same Act forbids, among other things, the use as a trade mark of anything identical or similar to the mark of an international organisation, in a way that suggests a connection with it.
Nusquama does not apply to register a trade mark.
It does not use the flag or the emblem as a trade mark, and sells nothing.
The lists of arms, insignia and marks that the Minister designates and the ordinance sets were not fetched. What was compared is the shapes and colours of the files.

### When a notice comes

1. A notice comes by the form for rights holders that "For rights holders" above gives, or by the steps under "Requests that cannot be made in public" in [Requests for removal, correction or withdrawal](./requests.en.md).
2. The operator reads it.
3. While deciding, the operator may add a note where the name appears, saying that Nusquama is not connected with the rights holder.
4. If the right is confirmed, the operator changes the title and the text of the site, the name of the Vercel project and the address of the site, the name of the repository, the forms and the README. GitHub sends links to the old name of a repository on to the new one.
5. The operator also changes the article on MicroWiki and the name of the account on X, as far as each service allows.
6. Some things are not rewritten. The rows of the King's errata and of the timeline, past issues of the Gazette and the history of the repository keep the old name. Where the site shows them, it adds a note that the name was changed.
7. Every change is recorded in the [timeline](../README.md) and the Gazette. The decision is written in the issue.
8. If the right is not confirmed, the operator gives the reason in the same place.

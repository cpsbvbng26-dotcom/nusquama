# Legality Statement

Last updated: 2026-11-13.

This is an English translation. The [Japanese version](./legality.md) governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

## What this page is

This page sets out, law by law, why the operator believes Nusquama breaks no law.
For each law it lists what must be present for the law to apply (the elements) next to the facts of Nusquama, and shows that no fact meets the elements.
It also shows where in the pages the rules that keep those facts true are written.

This page sets out the operator's understanding; it is neither legal advice nor a guarantee.
It has not been reviewed by a lawyer.
The statutes are summarised, not quoted, and the law names are given in common English renderings.
The summaries were checked on 2026-11-08 against the current texts obtained from the e-Gov law API. The articles checked are listed in each heading.
On 2026-11-12 this page was reviewed again, article by article. The articles added in that review are named in each section under "Added in the review", and were fetched and read on the same day.
The record of the review is in [Legal review record (2026-11-12)](https://github.com/cpsbvbng26-dotcom/nusquama/blob/main/docs/legal-audit-2026-11-12.md) (in Japanese).
At the end of each section, under "Text of", the wording itself is quoted.
The Japanese articles are quoted unchanged from the texts obtained from the e-Gov law API and recorded by the watch.
Foreign treaties, laws and explanations are quoted from the texts fetched on 2026-11-12 from the pages that publish them, without translation.
If the law or the facts change, this page will be revised.

## How Nusquama is run

Nusquama means no harm. It does not aim to deceive anyone or to hurt anyone.
The operator runs Nusquama in compliance with the laws of Japan and with the terms of the services it uses.
If the operator learns that something may break a law, the operator stops that part first, then checks it and fixes it.
When it is unclear whether something is lawful, the operator chooses not to do it.
This stance is promised in the [Terms of Use](./terms.en.md), under "No ill intent, and obeying the law".

## The facts relied on

The analysis below rests on the following facts. Each can be checked in the public repository and on the site.

- It has no territory and claims no land.
- It has no people, government, army or police.
- It issues no currency, passports, identity documents or certificates.
- It receives no money or goods. It sells nothing, takes no donations and shows no advertising.
- It makes and hands out no physical decorations, badges or uniforms.
- Its titles are words inside the fiction, used only in the repository, on the site, and in the articles and posts the King wrote to introduce Nusquama (listed in the README under "ほかの場所").
- The only personal information it handles is a GitHub account name, a link to the public correction, and the description the person writes. The linked page may reveal a real name or affiliation, and the form says so.
- Issues reporting mistakes and issues making requests handle the GitHub account name and what the person writes.
- It accepts no applications from people under 16, or from people whose country of residence forbids receiving knightly titles conferred by private persons.
- It collects no documents to check nationality, age, country of residence or real name. Age, country of residence, the country the applicant is in when applying, and nationality are taken from the applicant's own declaration, made by ticking the oaths in the form.
- Every page states that it is fiction and unrelated to anything real.

## Law by law

### Insurrection (Penal Code, Article 77)

- **Elements, in summary**: rioting with the purpose of destroying the governing machinery of the state, excluding state power over its territory and exercising power there, or otherwise subverting the basic constitutional order of government. What counts as a riot (many people joining to use violence or threats to the point of disturbing the peace of a locality) is not in the text; it comes from the usual interpretation.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it has no territory and does not try to exclude the government or power of Japan or any other country. There is no gathering of people, no violence and no threat. Article 9 of the Basic Law says the kingdom draws no sword and settles disputes with words and evidence.
- **Why the law does not apply**: there is neither the purpose nor a riot.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is"; Basic Law, Articles 1 and 9.

### False titles and insignia (Minor Offenses Act, Article 1, item 15)

- **Elements, in summary**: falsely claiming an official post, court rank or decoration, academic degree or other title set by law, or a foreign equivalent of these; or, without the right to do so, using a uniform, decoration, badge or other insignia set by law, or anything made to look like one.
- **Nusquama's facts**: Knight-King and knight are words inside a work of fiction, set by no country's law. Because Nusquama is not a state, its titles are not "foreign equivalents" either. It makes no physical decorations, badges or uniforms, and its emblem imitates no real insignia.
- **Why the law does not apply**: the titles used are not titles set by law, and no physical insignia are used. Using the titles in a way that could be mistaken for a legal title is itself forbidden.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Titles" (no use that could be mistaken for a legal title; nothing on CVs, business cards or official documents); Basic Law, Article 8; [Names and rights](./names.en.md), "The emblem"; [Rules on Knighting](./knighting.en.md), "The premise of good sense".

### Official documents and passports (Penal Code, Article 155; Passport Act, Article 23)

- **Elements, in summary**: forging, for use, a document that a public office or public official is to make (Penal Code, Article 155). Using a passport in another person's name, or handing over, receiving or holding a forged passport for use, among others (Passport Act, Article 23).
- **Nusquama's facts**: it makes no passports, identity documents, certificates or letters of appointment. Knighting only adds an account name to the roll and issues no document.
- **Why the law does not apply**: no document that could be forged is made.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is" (no passports, no nationality); [Rules on Knighting](./knighting.en.md), "What knighting is" (no documents or objects).
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: the "public office" and "public officer" of Article 155 of the Penal Code are, under Article 7 of the same Code, Japan's national and local officials and the places where they work. A paper made in Nusquama's name is not a public document in that sense. But making, with intent to use it, a document on rights, duties or the proof of facts in another person's name comes near the forgery of a private document (Penal Code, Article 159). A knight or third party who makes a "Nusquama passport" or the like in the name of the King or the kingdom comes near that article.

### Fraud (Penal Code, Article 246)

- **Elements, in summary**: deceiving a person into handing over property, or by that means obtaining an unlawful financial benefit.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it receives no money or goods. It sells no titles, takes no donations, and knighting is free.
- **Why the law does not apply**: no property or benefit changes hands.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Fees"; Basic Law, Article 6; the fifth of the ten precepts. The Terms also say that anyone claiming to grant Nusquama's titles for a fee has nothing to do with Nusquama.
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: if a third party sells titles in Nusquama's name and the operator does not help, the operator is not an accessory either (Penal Code, Article 62). What the Terms say about such people is not the reason for this conclusion. Once the operator learns of it, the operator states that it has no connection with Nusquama ("Acts outside the rules that come near a law's elements").

### Trade (Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, Article 2; Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, Article 5; Consumer Contract Act, Articles 2, 8 and 10)

- **Elements, in summary**: a seller or service provider selling goods or services door to door, by mail order, by telephone solicitation and so on (Act on Specified Commercial Transactions). A business making representations about its own goods or services that mislead people into thinking they are much better or more favourable than they are (premiums and representations act). In a contract between a consumer and a person acting as a business, a clause that fully excludes the business's liability for damages, among others, is void (Consumer Contract Act).
- **Nusquama's facts**: it sells nothing, shows no advertising and runs no business for profit.
- **Why the law does not apply**: the transactions these laws assume do not exist. In case the Consumer Contract Act ever applies, the "Liability" section of the [Disclaimer](./disclaimer.en.md) says that the exclusion does not apply and that the operator is liable, including for slight negligence. Article 8(1) of that Act voids full exclusions of liability, and Article 8(3) voids partial exclusions that do not make clear they apply only to slight negligence. The Disclaimer falls under neither.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Fees".
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: under the premiums and representations act, a "representation" is an advertisement or other representation a business makes about the trade in goods or services it supplies itself (Article 2(4) of that Act). Nusquama has no such trade.
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: under the Consumer Contract Act, a "business" is a corporation or other body, or an individual who becomes a party to a contract as a business or for a business (Article 2(2) of that Act). The text does not say that a business must be run for profit. Whether running Nusquama, which repeatedly takes applications from anyone and obtains agreement to the Terms, counts as acting "as a business" cannot be settled from the text. The operator treats the Act as applying, and checked it article by article. Article 8(1) voids not only full exclusions of liability but also partial exclusions of liability for intent or gross negligence (items 2 and 4); the Disclaimer leaves intent and gross negligence outside the exclusion, and does not apply the exclusion where the Act applies. No clause falls under Article 8-2 (waiving the right to cancel) or Article 8-3 (cancellation because of guardianship and the like). On Article 9 (liquidated damages and penalties), the Terms say no penalty and no damages beyond what the law provides are asked for. The clauses to be weighed against Article 10 are the agreed court and the clause on changes. The agreed court is not exclusive, and a change to anyone's disadvantage is shown before it takes effect, so the operator treats neither as a clause that harms a consumer's interests one-sidedly. The Terms say they restrict no right that the law does not allow to be restricted.

### Standard terms (Civil Code, Articles 548-2, 548-3 and 548-4)

- **Elements, in summary**: a person who agrees to enter into a standard transaction is deemed to agree to each clause of the standard terms, for example when agreeing that the standard terms form the contract (Article 548-2(1)). A clause found to harm the other party's interests one-sidedly is deemed not agreed (Article 548-2(2)). On request, the content of the standard terms is shown (Article 548-3). The terms may be changed when the change suits the general interest of the other party, or when it does not go against the purpose of the contract and is reasonable; the time the change takes effect and the new content are made known on the internet or by other means before it takes effect (Article 548-4).
- **Nusquama's facts**: people applying for knighting agree, in the form, to the version of the Terms of that day. People who only read do not agree. All the pages are public in the repository and on the site. A change against readers' or knights' interests is shown in the Terms of Use, with what changes and the day it takes effect, before it takes effect.
- **Why the law does not apply**: whether a free application for knighting is a "standard transaction" cannot be settled from the text. In case it is, agreement is obtained in the form, the content is public, and changes are shown before they take effect. The clauses to be weighed against Article 548-2(2) are also the agreed court and the clause on changes; for the same reasons as under Article 10 of the Consumer Contract Act, the operator treats neither as harming the other party's interests one-sidedly.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Who these terms cover" and "Changes and ending".

### Trademarks and indications (Trademark Act, Article 25; Unfair Competition Prevention Act, Article 2, paragraph 1, items 1 and 2)

- **Elements, in summary**: using, without right, a registered trademark for the designated goods or services, which the trademark owner has the exclusive right to use (Trademark Act). Using an indication identical or similar to another person's indication of goods or business that is widely known among consumers, causing confusion; or using, as one's own indication, something identical or similar to another person's famous indication (Unfair Competition Prevention Act).
- **Nusquama's facts**: it does no business. Its names come from ordinary Latin words and words from the classics, and borrow no one's indication of goods or organisation. The emblem is the operator's own work.
- **Why the law does not apply**: there is no use in business, and no one else's indication is used.
- **Rules that keep this true**: [Names and rights](./names.en.md); Disclaimer, "Relation to real things".
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: under the Trademark Act, a "trademark" is a mark used by a person who produces or transfers goods, or provides services, as a business, on those goods or services (Article 2(1) of that Act). The way Nusquama uses its names, with no goods or services in business, is considered to be outside that definition. Whether the "business" of Article 2(1), items 1 and 2, of the Unfair Competition Prevention Act must be run for profit cannot be settled from the text. Nusquama uses no one else's indication of goods or business, so the operator treats those items as not met, whether or not it counts as a business.

### Copyright (Copyright Act, Articles 21 and 23)

- **Elements, in summary**: reproducing a work, or transmitting it to the public (including making it transmittable), which the author has the exclusive right to do, without permission.
- **Nusquama's facts**: the text and the emblem were written with Claude Code at the operator's request and checked by the operator. No one else's work is copied, except the texts of treaties, laws, terms and explanations quoted under "Text of" on this page. Utopia and the Zhuangzi are named only as sources of the names, and none of their text is copied. Both are long out of copyright.
- **Why the law does not apply**: the pages carry no one else's work except quotations, and the repository, too, keeps of texts other than laws, treaties and texts of state organs only the passages quoted and the passages its checks rely on. The quotations are checked against Article 32 of the Copyright Act in the next item.
- **Rules that keep this true**: [Names and rights](./names.en.md); [Requests for removal, correction or withdrawal](./requests.en.md) (requests to remove content that infringes a right).
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: Japanese laws are not the subject of rights, under Article 13, item 1, of the Copyright Act. Whether foreign laws and treaties are "laws" in that item cannot be settled from the text. So the foreign texts, the explanation of the California Attorney General and Vercel's documents are treated as quotations (Article 32(1) of that Act). The elements of that paragraph are a work made public, a quotation compatible with fair practice, and a quotation within a scope justified by its purpose. The quoted texts come from public pages; only the passage needed to show what this page relies on is quoted, in a block set apart from the text, with a link to the page it comes from (the indication of source under Article 48(1), item 1). The operator therefore considers the elements met. What "fair practice" and a "justified scope" contain cannot be settled from the text; the operator treats quotations in this form as meeting both. If text written with a language model is found to resemble someone else's text, that text is stopped first, then checked and corrected.

### Personal information (Act on the Protection of Personal Information, Articles 17, 18, 27 and 28)

- **Elements, in summary**: a business handling personal information must specify the purpose of use as far as possible and not handle the information beyond what that purpose needs. Before giving personal data to a third party, or to a third party in a foreign country, it must obtain the person's consent, among other rules.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it handles only the GitHub account name, the link to the correction and the description the person writes, and asks for no real name, address or contact details. It states the purpose of use, that the information is public, and that it is handled by GitHub and Vercel in the United States, and the person consents in the application form. Withdrawal from the roll and removal are granted at any time, free of charge.
- **Why the law does not apply**: the handling the law requires is met by the pages and the form. The name and address under Article 32 are given at a contact the person has made public on their profile; a person with none can receive them by making one public for a while.
- **Duty by duty**: the duties of Articles 17, 18, 20–23, 25–28, 32–38 and 40 of the Act, Article 10 of its Cabinet Order and Articles 7, 8, 10 and 17 of its Rules are paired, paragraph by paragraph, with the sentences that meet them, in `verification/duties.json`. The check confirms that every sentence exists and that every article is watched.
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: a business handling personal information is anyone who uses a personal information database for a business, other than state organs and the like (Article 16(2) of the Act). Profit is not an element. The roll is a table of account names in the order of knighting, and the linked correction may reveal who the person is (Article 2(1) of the Act). The operator treats itself as a business handling personal information. There are no employees, and only the operator's account can change the roll (Article 24 of the Act). Listing on the roll is considered a provision to an unspecified large number of people. Its record (Article 29 of the Act; Articles 19 and 20 of its Rules) is the repository history in which the row was added. The retention period follows Article 21 of the Rules. When a name is removed from the roll on request, the history in which the row was added remains ([Requests](./requests.en.md), "What cannot be removed"). No personal data is received from third parties, so the confirmation of Article 30 of the Act does not arise.
- **Rules that keep this true**: [Privacy](./privacy.en.md); [Rules on Knighting](./knighting.en.md), "Withdrawal and revocation"; the pledges in the application form.

### Reputation and credit (Penal Code, Articles 230, 231 and 233; Civil Code, Article 709)

- **Elements, in summary**: publicly alleging facts that damage a person's reputation; publicly insulting a person, even without alleging facts; damaging a person's credit by spreading false rumours or by deception; infringing, intentionally or negligently, another person's rights or legally protected interests and so causing damage.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it discusses no particular person. The roll of knights lists only account names, on the person's own application and consent. When an application is refused, the reason is given briefly and the person is not judged.
- **Why the law does not apply**: nothing damages anyone's reputation or credit.
- **What others write**: applicants and people making requests write text in issues. If something that harms another person's reputation or exposes their personal information is written there, the operator takes it down at once, before deciding anything else.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What is not allowed" (no defamation or harassment); [Requests for removal, correction or withdrawal](./requests.en.md), "After a request".
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: issues are written on GitHub, where anyone may read them. Such a transmission is a "specified telecommunication" under Article 2, item 1, of the Act on Responses to Rights Infringement on Information Distribution Platforms. Whether the operator is a provider of that service (item 4 of the same Article) cannot be settled from the text, because GitHub holds the equipment; the operator treats itself as one. As such, it is not liable for a post that infringes another person's rights unless it was technically possible to stop the transmission and it knew of the infringement, or there were reasonable grounds to find that it could have known (Article 3(1) of that Act). For harm to the writer caused by taking a post down, it is not liable when the takedown went no further than necessary and, among other cases, there were reasonable grounds to believe another person's rights were being unjustly infringed (Article 3(2)). The operator takes such text down at once, before deciding anything else, and afterwards checks whether taking it down was right. The duties of large-scale providers (Article 2, item 14) belong to those designated, and do not reach the operator.

### Telecommunications business (Telecommunications Business Act, Article 164)

- **Elements, in summary**: a telecommunications business is a business providing, to meet the demand of others, a telecommunications service, that is, relaying others' communications with telecommunications equipment or otherwise making such equipment available for others' communications (Article 2, items 3 and 4). Whoever runs one registers (Article 9) or notifies (Article 16(1)). But the Act does not apply, except for the provisions listed in Article 164(3), to a business that provides a service other than relaying others' communications without installing telecommunications circuit facilities (Article 164(1), item 3).
- **Nusquama's facts**: applications, requests and reports are received in GitHub issues. Vercel serves the site. The operator installs no telecommunications circuit facilities and relays no communications between users; an issue is a public post, not a communication carried to a particular recipient. The operator has not been designated by the Minister.
- **Why the law does not apply**: even if receiving issues were "making equipment available for others' communications", it is not relaying and no circuit facilities are installed, so it would be a business under Article 164(1), item 3, to which the notification provisions do not apply. It can also be said that GitHub, not the operator, makes the equipment available. The Ministry's entry manual (supplement) treats a website that sends only its own information, and a form receiving inquiries, as not a telecommunications business (see the review record).
- **Rules that keep this true**: [Requests](./requests.en.md), "How to ask" (GitHub is the only channel).

### Status under international law

- **Elements, in summary**: under international law, a state is generally expected to have a permanent population, a defined territory, a government, and the capacity to enter into relations with other states (Montevideo Convention of 1933, Article 1). Article 3 of the same convention says that the political existence of a state is independent of recognition by other states. The convention is among American states, and Japan is not a party to it, but it is widely cited for the criteria of statehood. The text was checked on 2026-11-12 in the [English text of the Avalon Project (Yale Law School)](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/20th_century/intam03.asp), and the parties in the [list of the Organization of American States](https://www.oas.org/juridico/english/sigs/a-40.html), which holds the ratifications.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it has no population, territory or government, and claims no sovereignty and no land. It claims no state's territory, no land that belongs to no state, and no structure at sea. It enters into no diplomatic relations and seeks no recognition as a state. It issues no passports and no nationality.
- **Why the law does not apply**: it meets none of the four criteria and does not try to. Nusquama is not a state under international law and holds no rights or duties under it. It impairs no state's sovereignty, territorial integrity or status under international law.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is"; Disclaimer, "A work of fiction"; Basic Law, Article 1.

### Laws outside Japan

- Nusquama is a non-profit work of fiction run from Japan. It does no business and no trade in any country, and solicits no money and no paid membership.
- Some countries forbid falsely claiming their honours or titles. Nusquama forbids using its titles outside the kingdom and any use that could be mistaken for a real title or honour (Basic Law, Article 8; Terms of Use, "Titles"). The premise of good sense (Rules on Knighting) does the same.
- For example, Article 8 of Italian Law No. 178 of 1951 forbids private persons and bodies from conferring knightly distinctions under any name. The same article also punishes anyone who uses such a distinction, and the penalty for use applies even when it was conferred abroad. The text was checked on 2026-11-08 in [Normattiva](https://www.normattiva.it/uri-res/N2Ls?urn:nir:stato:legge:1951-03-03;178~art8), Italy's official database of legislation. Applications from people living in such countries are not accepted ([Rules on Knighting](./knighting.en.md), "Who may apply").
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: what the article forbids is conferring by private persons and bodies; the recipient's country of residence is not an element. Whether the penalty for conferring (six months to two years) reaches a foreigner who confers abroad is not settled by Articles 7, 8 and 10 of the Italian Penal Code; the operator keeps applications paused until this is checked (Rules on Knighting, "Pause in receiving applications"). Article 6 treats a crime as committed in Italy when part of the act or its result occurs there. Whether entering, in Japan, the name of a person in Japan on the roll falls under it cannot be settled from the text; the operator treats it as not falling under it. The penalty for use is a fine (ammenda) only, and the law is not in the list of exclusions to Article 1 of Legislative Decree No. 8 of 2016, so it reads as having become an administrative penalty.
- The EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) can reach those outside the EU, for example when they offer services to people in the EU. In case it does, Nusquama handles only what the person makes public themselves, takes it with consent, and answers requests for access, correction and erasure free of charge ([Privacy](./privacy.en.md)). Article 3(2) of the GDPR applies to a controller outside the EU where it offers goods or services, irrespective of payment, to people in the EU, among other cases. The text was checked on 2026-11-08 in [EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32016R0679).
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: where the GDPR applies under Article 3(2), a controller outside the EU designates in writing a representative in the EU (GDPR, Article 27). That duty does not apply to processing that is occasional, does not include large-scale processing of special categories of data, and is unlikely to result in a risk to the rights and freedoms of natural persons (paragraph 2(a) of that Article). Nusquama does not offer its service to people in the EU, and from 2026-11-22 it accepts no application from anyone outside Japan. Even if the GDPR applies, the operator considers the handling of the roll to fall under paragraph 2(a), and has designated no representative. Claude decided this on 2026-11-12, because the roll handles only account names and summaries of corrections, and asks for no special categories of data.
- The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) covers businesses run for profit. Nusquama runs no business for profit, so the operator considers that it does not apply. This was checked on 2026-11-08 on the [CCPA page of the California Attorney General](https://oag.ca.gov/privacy/ccpa), which says the Act covers for-profit businesses and generally does not apply to nonprofit organisations. The statute itself (Civil Code section 1798.140(d)) was read on 2026-11-12 in an Internet Archive copy, since the State's page blocks automated access; it makes the purpose of profit an element.
- From 2026-11-22, only people who live in Japan and are in Japan when they apply may apply. The laws of other countries cannot all be checked, so people who live where one of them might reach are not accepted at all. Some of those laws reach people who are in the country, not only those who live there, so an application made while abroad, for travel, work or study, is not accepted either ([Rules on Knighting](./knighting.en.md), "Who may apply").
- From 2026-11-22, applications from people who hold Italian nationality (including dual nationals) are not accepted either. The reasons are that, by plain common sense, an Italian national becoming a knight of a Japanese micronation is out of place to begin with, and that the article's penalty for use reaches an Italian national who uses a title conferred abroad.
- From 2026-11-22, applications from people who hold the nationality of a country that restricts accepting foreign titles are not accepted either ([Rules on Knighting](./knighting.en.md), "Countries that restrict foreign titles").
- Under these limits, and the rule in the Rules on Knighting on the laws of the country of one's nationality, the operator treats laws outside Japan as not reaching knighting.
- The applicant's declaration is not checked, but if the linked correction states clearly that the applicant does not meet the conditions, the operator asks the applicant, and does not knight them unless they are confirmed to meet the conditions. This keeps what could have been noticed from being overlooked ([Rules on Knighting](./knighting.en.md), "What the operator checks").

## How this was reviewed, and how the premises could fail

The conclusion of this page was checked in the five ways below.

1. **Elements as logic**: an offence or a duty arises only when all of its elements are present. Writing the elements as A₁ ∧ A₂ ∧ … ∧ Aₙ, one fact that denies a single Aᵢ makes the whole false. For each law, `verification/legal-matrix.json` lists which element is denied by which sentence, and the check confirms that the sentence exists.
2. **Coverage**: every pair of actor (operator, applicant, knight, reader, third party) and act (publishing, receiving applications, listing on the roll, using titles, keeping information, reaching abroad) is listed in `verification/legal-matrix.json`. Every pair is either covered by an item on this page or cannot occur.
3. **Consistency**: the pages were compared for the same matter being written differently. The extent of "inside the kingdom" differed from page to page, so it is now defined once, in the Terms of Use, and the other pages point there (the King's errata, entry 6).
4. **Fault tree**: the ways the facts relied on could fail were listed, likelihood and severity were each estimated from 1 to 3, and the product was used to rank them. The numbers are the operator's estimates and serve only to rank.
5. **Breaking on purpose**: each fixed sentence was altered in turn, and the check was seen to fail.

This method works within the elements set out.

## How the agreement on courts works

The Terms of Use put settlement by agreement first, and then name an agreed court of first instance. This section says how far that clause reaches.

- **The law, in summary**: the parties may, by agreement, choose the court of first instance. The agreement has no effect unless it concerns claims arising from a defined legal relationship and is made in writing or as an electronic record (Code of Civil Procedure, Article 11). An agreement on which country's courts will hear future disputes over a consumer contract has effect only in limited cases, such as when it allows claims in the courts of the country where the consumer lives (Code of Civil Procedure, Article 3-7, paragraph 5).
- **Nusquama's facts**: people applying for knighting tick their agreement to the Terms of Use in the form, and the tick stays in the issue. People who only read the site or the repository tick nothing.
- **How it works**: the agreement is made only with those who agreed in the form, only for suits based on the Terms or on their own application, listing on the roll or withdrawal, and is not exclusive. A record of the agreement remains as an electronic record. There is no written agreement with those who only read; for them, and for other disputes, the court set by law has jurisdiction. The operator sues only in the Utsunomiya courts or at the place of the other party's general forum. The Terms state that they do not take away the right of a consumer, or of a person outside Japan, to sue in another court where the law allows it.
- **Added in the review (2026-11-12)**: the tick in the form points to where the rules are; it does not copy their text. Which version was agreed is checked by the date of the application and the repository history. If a request to delete the issue is granted, the record of agreement goes with it. Even the agreed court may transfer a case to another competent court where needed for fairness between the parties (Code of Civil Procedure, Article 17). A consumer may sue in Japan if the consumer's domicile is in Japan (Code of Civil Procedure, Article 3-4(1)). How the agreed court is treated where the Consumer Contract Act applies is set out under "Trade".
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Governing law and disputes"; the pledges in the application form.

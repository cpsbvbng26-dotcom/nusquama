# Legality Statement

Last updated: 2026-11-08.

This is an English translation. The [Japanese version](./legality.md) governs; if the two differ, the Japanese version applies.

## What this page is

This page sets out, law by law, why the operator believes Nusquama breaks no law.
For each law it lists what must be present for the law to apply (the elements) next to the facts of Nusquama, and shows that no fact meets the elements.
It also shows where in the pages the rules that keep those facts true are written.

This page sets out the operator's understanding; it is neither legal advice nor a guarantee.
It has not been reviewed by a lawyer.
The statutes are summarised, not quoted, and the law names are given in common English renderings.
The summaries were checked on 2026-11-08 against the current texts obtained from the e-Gov law API. The articles checked are listed in each heading.
The texts were obtained by querying the e-Gov law API (v1) from GitHub Actions.
The original texts can be read on [e-Gov Laws Search](https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/) (in Japanese).
If the law or the facts change, this page will be revised.

## How Nusquama is run

Nusquama means no harm. It does not aim to deceive anyone or to hurt anyone.
The operator runs Nusquama in compliance with the laws of Japan and with the terms of the services it uses.
If the operator learns that something may break a law, the operator stops that part first, then checks it and fixes it.
When it is unclear whether something is lawful, the operator chooses not to do it.
This stance is promised in the [Terms of Use](./terms.en.md), under "No ill intent, and obeying the law".

## The facts relied on

The analysis below rests on the following facts. Each can be checked in the public repository and on the site.

- It has no territory and claims no land.
- It has no people, government, army or police.
- It issues no currency, passports, identity documents or certificates.
- It receives no money or goods. It sells nothing, takes no donations and shows no advertising.
- It makes and hands out no physical decorations, badges or uniforms.
- Its titles are words inside the fiction, used only in the repository, on the site, and in the MicroWiki article the King wrote.
- The only personal information it handles is a GitHub account name, a link to the public correction, and the description the person writes. The linked page may reveal a real name or affiliation, and the form says so.
- It accepts no applications from people under 16, or from people whose country of residence forbids receiving knightly titles conferred by private persons.
- It collects no documents to check nationality, age, country of residence or real name. Age and country of residence are taken from the applicant's own declaration, made by ticking the oaths in the form.
- Every page states that it is fiction and unrelated to anything real.

## Law by law

### Insurrection (Penal Code, Article 77)

- **Elements, in summary**: rioting with the purpose of destroying the governing machinery of the state, excluding state power over its territory and exercising power there, or otherwise subverting the basic constitutional order of government. What counts as a riot (many people joining to use violence or threats to the point of disturbing the peace of a locality) is not in the text; it comes from the usual interpretation.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it has no territory and does not try to exclude the government or power of Japan or any other country. There is no gathering of people, no violence and no threat. Article 9 of the Basic Law says the kingdom draws no sword and settles disputes with words and evidence.
- **Why the law does not apply**: there is neither the purpose nor a riot.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is"; Basic Law, Articles 1 and 9.

### False titles and insignia (Minor Offenses Act, Article 1, item 15)

- **Elements, in summary**: falsely claiming an official post, court rank or decoration, academic degree or other title set by law, or a foreign equivalent of these; or, without the right to do so, using a uniform, decoration, badge or other insignia set by law, or anything made to look like one.
- **Nusquama's facts**: Knight-King and knight are words inside a work of fiction, set by no country's law. Because Nusquama is not a state, its titles are not "foreign equivalents" either. It makes no physical decorations, badges or uniforms, and its emblem imitates no real insignia.
- **Why the law does not apply**: the titles used are not titles set by law, and no physical insignia are used. Using the titles in a way that could be mistaken for a legal title is itself forbidden.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Titles" (no use that could be mistaken for a legal title; nothing on CVs, business cards or official documents); Basic Law, Article 8; Names and Rights, "Emblem" (in Japanese).

### Official documents and passports (Penal Code, Article 155; Passport Act, Article 23)

- **Elements, in summary**: forging, for use, a document that a public office or public official is to make (Penal Code, Article 155). Using a passport in another person's name, or handing over, receiving or holding a forged passport for use, among others (Passport Act, Article 23).
- **Nusquama's facts**: it makes no passports, identity documents, certificates or letters of appointment. Knighting only adds an account name to the roll and issues no document.
- **Why the law does not apply**: no document that could be forged is made.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is" (no passports, no nationality); Knighting Rules, "What knighting is" (no documents or objects; in Japanese).

### Fraud (Penal Code, Article 246)

- **Elements, in summary**: deceiving a person into handing over property, or by that means obtaining an unlawful financial benefit.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it receives no money or goods. It sells no titles, takes no donations, and knighting is free.
- **Why the law does not apply**: no property or benefit changes hands.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Fees"; Basic Law, Article 6; the fifth of the ten precepts. The Terms also say that anyone claiming to grant Nusquama's titles for a fee has nothing to do with Nusquama.

### Trade (Act on Specified Commercial Transactions, Article 2; Act against Unjustifiable Premiums and Misleading Representations, Article 5; Consumer Contract Act, Articles 2, 8 and 10)

- **Elements, in summary**: a seller or service provider selling goods or services door to door, by mail order, by telephone solicitation and so on (Act on Specified Commercial Transactions). A business making representations about its own goods or services that mislead people into thinking they are much better or more favourable than they are (premiums and representations act). In a contract between a consumer and a person acting as a business, a clause that fully excludes the business's liability for damages, among others, is void (Consumer Contract Act).
- **Nusquama's facts**: it sells nothing, shows no advertising and runs no business.
- **Why the law does not apply**: the transactions these laws assume do not exist. In case the Consumer Contract Act ever applies, the "Liability" section of the [Disclaimer](./disclaimer.en.md) says that the exclusion does not apply and that the operator is liable, including for slight negligence. Article 8(1) of that Act voids full exclusions of liability, and Article 8(3) voids partial exclusions that do not make clear they apply only to slight negligence. The Disclaimer falls under neither.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Fees".

### Currency and means of payment (Payment Services Act, Article 3)

- **Elements, in summary**: issuing prepaid payment instruments, that is, tokens or electronic records issued in return for payment matching an amount or a quantity, which can be used to pay for, or to claim, goods or services.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it issues no currency, points, vouchers or electronic value.
- **Why the law does not apply**: nothing is issued or handled.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What Nusquama is" (no currency).

### Trademarks and indications (Trademark Act, Article 25; Unfair Competition Prevention Act, Article 2, paragraph 1, items 1 and 2)

- **Elements, in summary**: using, without right, a registered trademark for the designated goods or services, which the trademark owner has the exclusive right to use (Trademark Act). Using an indication identical or similar to another person's indication of goods or business that is widely known among consumers, causing confusion; or using, as one's own indication, something identical or similar to another person's famous indication (Unfair Competition Prevention Act).
- **Nusquama's facts**: it does no business. Its names come from ordinary Latin words and words from the classics, and borrow no one's indication of goods or organisation. The emblem is the operator's own work.
- **Why the law does not apply**: there is no use in business, and no one else's indication is used.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Names and Rights (in Japanese); Disclaimer, "Relation to real things".

### Copyright (Copyright Act, Articles 21 and 23)

- **Elements, in summary**: reproducing a work, or transmitting it to the public (including making it transmittable), which the author has the exclusive right to do, without permission.
- **Nusquama's facts**: the text and the emblem were written with Claude Code at the operator's request and checked by the operator. No one else's work is copied. Utopia and the Zhuangzi are named only as sources of the names, and none of their text is copied. Both are long out of copyright.
- **Why the law does not apply**: no one else's work is reproduced.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Names and Rights (in Japanese); Requests for Removal, Correction or Withdrawal (requests to remove content that infringes a right; in Japanese).

### Personal information (Act on the Protection of Personal Information, Articles 17, 18, 27 and 28)

- **Elements, in summary**: a business handling personal information must specify the purpose of use as far as possible and not handle the information beyond what that purpose needs. Before giving personal data to a third party, or to a third party in a foreign country, it must obtain the person's consent, among other rules.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it handles only the GitHub account name, the link to the correction and the description the person writes, and asks for no real name, address or contact details. It states the purpose of use, that the information is public, and that it is handled by GitHub and Vercel in the United States, and the person consents in the application form. Withdrawal from the roll and removal are granted at any time, free of charge.
- **Why the law does not apply**: the handling the law requires is met by the pages and the form.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Privacy Notice (in Japanese); Knighting Rules, "Withdrawal and revocation" (in Japanese); the oaths in the application form.

### Reputation and credit (Penal Code, Articles 230 and 231; Civil Code, Article 709)

- **Elements, in summary**: publicly alleging facts that damage a person's reputation; publicly insulting a person, even without alleging facts; infringing, intentionally or negligently, another person's rights or legally protected interests and so causing damage.
- **Nusquama's facts**: it discusses no particular person. The roll of knights lists only account names, on the person's own application and consent. When an application is refused, the reason is given briefly and the person is not judged.
- **Why the law does not apply**: nothing damages anyone's reputation.
- **What others write**: applicants and people making requests write text in issues. If something that harms another person's reputation or exposes their personal information is written there, the operator takes it down at once, before deciding anything else.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "What is not allowed" (no defamation or harassment); Requests for Removal, Correction or Withdrawal, "After a request is received" (in Japanese).

### Laws outside Japan

- Nusquama is a non-profit work of fiction run from Japan. It does no business and no trade in any country, and solicits no money and no paid membership.
- Some countries forbid falsely claiming their honours or titles. Nusquama forbids using its titles outside the kingdom and any use that could be mistaken for a real title or honour (Basic Law, Article 8; Terms of Use, "Titles").
- For example, Article 8 of Italian Law No. 178 of 1951 forbids private persons and bodies from conferring knightly distinctions under any name. This was confirmed from secondary sources; the original text was not checked. Applications from people living in such countries are not accepted (Knighting Rules, "Who may apply"; in Japanese). The country of residence is taken from the applicant's own declaration and is not checked (Knighting Rules, "What is not checked"; in Japanese).
- The EU General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) can reach those outside the EU, for example when they offer services to people in the EU. In case it does, Nusquama handles only what the person makes public themselves, takes it with consent, and answers requests for access, correction and erasure free of charge (Privacy Notice; in Japanese). The text of the GDPR was not checked; this rests on general knowledge.
- The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) covers businesses run for profit. Nusquama runs no business for profit, so the operator considers that it does not apply. This text was not checked either.
- The operator has not checked the laws of other countries one by one.

- **Vercel**: the site is served on Vercel's Hobby plan, which is limited to personal, non-commercial use. Nusquama is non-commercial and meets that condition.

## How this was reviewed, and the risks that remain

The conclusion of this page was checked in the five ways below.

1. **Elements as logic**: an offence or a duty arises only when all of its elements are present. Writing the elements as A₁ ∧ A₂ ∧ … ∧ Aₙ, one fact that denies a single Aᵢ makes the whole false. For each law, `verification/legal-matrix.json` lists which element is denied by which sentence, and the check confirms that the sentence exists.
2. **Coverage**: every pair of actor (operator, applicant, knight, reader, third party) and act (publishing, receiving applications, listing on the roll, using titles, keeping information, reaching abroad) is listed in `verification/legal-matrix.json`. Every pair is either covered by an item on this page or cannot occur.
3. **Consistency**: the pages were compared for the same matter being written differently. The extent of "inside the kingdom" differed from page to page, so it is now defined once, in the Terms of Use, and the other pages point there (the King's errata, entry 6).
4. **Fault tree**: the ways the facts relied on could fail were listed, likelihood and severity were each estimated from 1 to 3, and the product was used to rank them. The numbers are the operator's estimates and serve only to rank.
5. **Breaking on purpose**: each fixed sentence was altered in turn, and the check was seen to fail.

## How the agreement on courts works

The Terms of Use put settlement by agreement first, and then name an agreed court of first instance. This section says how far that clause reaches.

- **The law, in summary**: the parties may, by agreement, choose the court of first instance. The agreement has no effect unless it concerns claims arising from a defined legal relationship and is made in writing or as an electronic record (Code of Civil Procedure, Article 11). An agreement on which country's courts will hear future disputes over a consumer contract has effect only in limited cases, such as when it allows claims in the courts of the country where the consumer lives (Code of Civil Procedure, Article 3-7, paragraph 5).
- **Nusquama's facts**: people applying for knighting tick their agreement to the Terms of Use in the form, and the tick stays in the issue. People who only read the site or the repository tick nothing.
- **How it works**: with those who agreed in the form, a record of the agreement remains as an electronic record. With those who only read, there is no written agreement, so the agreement on courts may have no effect. In that case, the court set by law has jurisdiction. The Terms state that they do not take away the right of a consumer, or of a person outside Japan, to sue in another court where the law allows it.
- **Rules that keep this true**: Terms of Use, "Governing law and disputes"; the oaths in the application form.
